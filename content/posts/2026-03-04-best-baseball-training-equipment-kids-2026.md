## Buying Guide

The most common mistake parents make is buying equipment that's too large for their child. Youth baseball bats should allow the player to hold the bat straight out to the side with one hand without dropping it. Gloves should close comfortably without too much excess material.

Safety is paramount in baseball. Always ensure your child has a proper batting helmet with a face guard for younger players. For pitchers, monitor pitch counts and follow age-appropriate guidelines to protect growing arms.

Look for equipment that grows with your child. Adjustable tees, youth-to-adult transitional gloves, and training aids that work across skill levels provide better long-term value than equipment they'll outgrow in a season.

Quality matters more in baseball equipment than in many other sports. A well-made glove or bat will last several seasons and actually improve with use, while cheap alternatives often fall apart mid-season and can affect a child's development.
