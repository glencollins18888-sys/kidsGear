## Buying Guide

Every home batting practice setup should start with a quality tee and net. Even professional hitters take hundreds of tee swings daily. A good tee forces hitters to focus on mechanics without the variable of pitch timing, while a net provides a safe backstop.

Pitching machines range from basic to advanced. For most youth players, a soft-toss machine throwing lightweight balls is ideal because it allows backyard use without a full batting cage. Save hard-ball machines for when your child has access to a caged facility.

Net quality is a safety issue, not just convenience. Cheap nets with thin frames can collapse or allow balls to punch through, creating hazards. Look for nets with reinforced netting and sturdy frames that maintain tension even after thousands of hits.

Consider your available space realistically. A 7x7 net, tee, and enough room for a full swing requires a minimum area of about 15x15 feet. If space is limited, a Hit-A-Way trainer on a pole takes up virtually no ground space while still providing excellent swing development.
