## Buying Guide

Always place gymnastics equipment on proper matting. Even low-height beams and bars should have padded surfaces underneath because kids fall in unexpected ways. A combination of thick tumbling mats directly under equipment and puzzle mats for the surrounding area creates a safe training zone.

Ceiling height is often overlooked when setting up home gymnastics equipment. Kip bars and even handstands require significant overhead clearance. Measure your ceiling height and compare it to the equipment's maximum height plus your child's height with arms extended before purchasing.

Start with the basics: a mat and one apparatus. Adding everything at once can be overwhelming and expensive. A quality mat with either a beam or bar provides months of productive practice. Expand your home gym as your child's commitment and skill level grow.

Check with your child's gymnastics coach before purchasing home equipment. Coaches can advise which skills are safe to practice at home and which should only be done under supervision at the gym. This prevents kids from attempting skills they're not ready for in an unsupervised environment.
