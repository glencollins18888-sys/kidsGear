## Introduction

Martial arts teach discipline, confidence, and self-defense, but sparring involves real contact. Proper protective gear is non-negotiable for any child participating in karate, taekwondo, jiu-jitsu, or boxing. The right gear absorbs impact, prevents injuries, and gives kids the confidence to practice techniques without fear.

Poorly fitting or low-quality gear can actually increase injury risk. Headgear that shifts during sparring blocks vision, loose shin guards slide out of position, and thin chest protectors don't absorb enough impact. Invest in quality — your child's safety is worth it.

## Buying Guide

Fit is the most critical factor for protective gear. Equipment that's too loose will shift during contact, creating blind spots and leaving areas unprotected. Gear that's too tight restricts movement and is uncomfortable, meaning kids won't want to wear it. Always measure according to the manufacturer's size chart and try gear on before heavy sparring.

Check your school's requirements before purchasing. Many dojos and martial arts programs have specific brand or certification requirements. Taekwondo programs typically require WTF-approved equipment, while boxing gyms may require USA Boxing approved headgear. Ask the instructor before shopping.

Replace gear when it shows signs of wear. Compressed foam loses its protective properties over time. If headgear feels less padded than when it was new, or chest protectors develop flat spots, it's time to replace them. Most protective gear lasts 1-2 years with regular use.

A proper mouthguard is the most important single piece of protective equipment. Dental injuries are expensive to fix and can affect a child for life. Never let a child spar without a properly fitted mouthguard, even in light contact drills.
