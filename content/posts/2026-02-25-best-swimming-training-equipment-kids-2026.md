## Buying Guide

Fit is everything in swim equipment. Goggles that leak distract kids and make them not want to swim. Fins that are too big cause blisters and cramps. Always refer to manufacturer size charts and when in doubt, try equipment on before committing to heavy training.

Safety must come first with any water activity. Training equipment is not a substitute for proper supervision. Even strong young swimmers should always be watched by an adult, and beginners should never use training equipment without direct supervision.

Start simple. Goggles and a kickboard are all a beginning swimmer needs. As your child advances and joins a swim team, their coach will recommend specific training aids for their development areas. Avoid buying specialized equipment until there's a clear need for it.

Chlorine is tough on swim gear. Rinse everything in fresh water after each use and let it dry completely. This simple habit will significantly extend the life of goggles, fins, and other equipment.
