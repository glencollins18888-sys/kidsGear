## Buying Guide

Start simple. An agility ladder and a set of cones provide enough equipment for hundreds of different drills. Only add specialized equipment like speed sleds and hurdles once your child has mastered basic ladder footwork patterns and shows genuine interest in speed training.

Surface matters for agility training. Grass provides natural cushioning and is ideal for most drills, but it should be relatively flat and free of holes. Turf fields are even better. Avoid training on concrete or asphalt, which is hard on growing joints and increases injury risk from falls.

Consistency beats intensity for young athletes. Short 15-20 minute agility sessions three times a week produce far better results than one exhausting hour-long workout. Keep sessions fun by turning drills into competitions or timing challenges.

Watch for signs of overtraining. Growing bodies need recovery time. If your child complains of persistent soreness, shin pain, or general fatigue, back off the intensity and frequency. Agility training should supplement their regular sport practice, not replace rest and recovery.
