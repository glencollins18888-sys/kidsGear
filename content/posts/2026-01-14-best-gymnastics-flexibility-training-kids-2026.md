## Introduction

Flexibility is arguably the most important physical attribute in gymnastics. Splits, bridges, leaps, and virtually every skill in the sport require exceptional range of motion. But flexibility must be developed safely — overstretching or forcing range of motion can cause serious injuries in growing bodies.

The best flexibility training tools provide gentle, progressive stretching assistance. They help young gymnasts hold positions longer, reach further gradually, and build the suppleness their sport demands. Here are our top picks for safe, effective flexibility development.

## Buying Guide

Never force flexibility. Growing bodies are more susceptible to stretching injuries than adult bodies because growth plates haven't closed yet. All stretching should feel like a gentle pull, never sharp pain. If a child says a stretch hurts (not just feels tight), stop immediately and reduce the intensity.

Consistency beats intensity. Five minutes of gentle stretching daily produces far better long-term results than one aggressive stretching session per week. Build stretching into the daily routine — after school, before bed, or while watching a show.

Warm up before stretching. Cold muscles don't stretch effectively and are more prone to strains. Have your child do 5-10 minutes of light activity (jogging, jumping jacks, or skipping) before any stretching session. The improved blood flow makes stretches both safer and more productive.

Active flexibility matters as much as passive flexibility. Being able to do a split on the ground is different from being able to hold a high leg kick in the air. Once basic passive flexibility is established, work on active flexibility — holding stretched positions using muscular strength alone.
