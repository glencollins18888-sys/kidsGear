## Introduction

Throwing events — shot put, discus, and javelin — are some of the most exciting parts of track and field. But standard competition implements are far too heavy and dangerous for young athletes. Youth-specific equipment is lighter, safer, and designed to teach proper mechanics without the injury risks.

Starting with the right weight builds technique first, strength second. A child who learns to throw a lighter implement with perfect form will eventually out-throw a child who muscled a heavier one with bad mechanics. These products prioritize safety and proper development.

## Buying Guide

Always match implement weight to age and ability. National federation guidelines specify: shot put at 4 lbs for youth (under 12) and 6 lbs for teens; discus at 1 kg for youth and 1.5 kg for teens; javelin at 300-400g for youth. Using implements that are too heavy teaches bad mechanics and risks injury.

Safety is paramount with throwing events. Always practice in an open area with no people downrange. Establish clear rules about when it's safe to retrieve implements. Never let kids throw toward each other, even with soft training equipment. Supervision is essential.

Focus on technique over distance. The natural instinct is to throw as far as possible, but throwing events are highly technical. A coach or quality instructional video should be part of the training plan. Equipment alone won't teach proper glide, spin, or approach techniques.

Consider starting with one event. Each throwing event has unique mechanics — shot put is linear, discus uses rotational power, and javelin requires a running approach. Let your child try each and see which feels most natural before investing in event-specific equipment.
